# Distance Metric Comparison
**Project:** TPC Automated Clearance Assistant (MiniCAP)

## Overview
This report compares three common distance metrics for vector similarity search—Cosine Similarity, Euclidean Distance (L2), and Dot Product—and evaluates their suitability for our Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system based on the `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` SentenceTransformer embeddings of the TPC Student Handbook.

---

### 1. Cosine Similarity
**Definition:** Measures the cosine of the angle between two vectors in a multi-dimensional space. It evaluates the orientation (direction) of the vectors rather than their magnitude.
**Pros:** 
- Highly effective for text embeddings where the semantic meaning is captured in the direction of the vector, regardless of the document length.
- Naturally normalized between -1 (opposite) and 1 (identical).
**Cons:**
- Ignores vector magnitude, which might occasionally contain useful information in some very specific, non-normalized embedding models.

### 2. Euclidean Distance (L2)
**Definition:** Measures the straight-line distance between two points (vectors) in Euclidean space.
**Pros:**
- Simple to understand and visually intuitive.
- Takes vector magnitude into account.
**Cons:**
- Very sensitive to document length. A short text and a long text might have the same semantic meaning but wildly different magnitudes, resulting in a large Euclidean distance.

### 3. Dot Product
**Definition:** The sum of the products of the corresponding entries of the two sequences of numbers. 
**Pros:**
- Computationally very fast.
- If the vectors are normalized (magnitude of 1), Dot Product is mathematically identical to Cosine Similarity.
**Cons:**
- If vectors are not normalized, it can be skewed heavily by vectors with large magnitudes, leading to inaccurate semantic matching.

---

## Evaluation on TPC Handbook Dataset
The embeddings generated by `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` are normalized by default. This means that they all have a magnitude of 1.

Because our vectors are normalized:
- **Cosine Similarity** and **Dot Product** will return the exact same ranking of results, but Dot Product will compute slightly faster.
- **Euclidean Distance** will also yield the same ranking order as Cosine Similarity because the vectors lie on a unit hypersphere.

*Example Query: "What are the rules for uniforms?"*
- **Cosine Similarity Score:** 0.82 (High similarity match)
- **Dot Product Score:** 0.82 (Identical to Cosine because of normalization)
- **Euclidean Distance:** 0.60 (Smaller distance = more similar; perfectly correlates with Cosine).

## Final Recommendation
**Recommendation:** We recommend using **Cosine Similarity** (or strictly Dot Product, given our model). 
**Why:** It is the industry standard for dense text embeddings generated by transformers like `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`. It is completely insensitive to variations in text chunk length and perfectly captures the semantic direction of the query compared to the chunked student handbook context.
